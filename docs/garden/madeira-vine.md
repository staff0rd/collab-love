# Madeira vine campaign

[*Anredera cordifolia*](https://weeds.dpi.nsw.gov.au/Weed/Madeiravine) — Weed of National Significance, illegal to sell in NSW, general biosecurity duty applies.

Reproduces entirely from tubers, not seed. A mature plant makes thousands: warty aerial ones at every stem node, plus underground ones up to 20cm across. Dense infestations leave up to 1,500 fallen aerial tubers per square metre, and they stay viable in soil for 5–10 years.

## Method

**1. Tarp first.** Lay tarps under any vine you're about to touch. Tubers detach at a nudge, and every one you miss is a new plant.

**2. Scrape and paint, don't rip down.** Scrape the stem back to the white fibrous layer and paint concentrated herbicide on within 15 seconds. Both sides, as high as you can reach, at staggered intervals. **Don't ringbark it** — that cuts the pathway the herbicide needs to reach the tubers. Treat before new underground tubers form.

**3. Leave the top growth to die in place.** Pulling live vine out of a tree showers tubers over everything below. Let it go brittle, then take it down over tarps. A cut vine left up there keeps dropping tubers for up to two years.

**4. Dig the underground tubers.** Any fragment left regrows.

**5. Double bag everything** in non-biodegradable plastic, to landfill. Not the compost, not the green bin, not a pile on the soil.

**6. Patrol — this is the campaign.** Walk it monthly through the growing season, pulling sprouts at two to four leaves while they're shallow. It runs for years, long after the clearing feels finished: one session without follow-up leaves you worse off, because the disturbance triggers vigorous regrowth. First thing in this project that warrants a recurring reminder in collab-love.

## Biological control

A leaf-feeding beetle, *Plectonycha correntina*, is approved for release in Australia. It depletes tuber reserves over time and suits frost- and flood-free sites. It works best **without** concurrent herbicide or physical removal, so it's an alternative to the above rather than an addition.

## Keep it off the bed

Never move soil from the madeira side to the flower bed.
